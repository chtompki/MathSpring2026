        \emph{What is Research?} The process of learning enough information to sufficiently answer a question.

        In Tuesday January 12th's seminar, David Chan opened the talk with a summary of the course generally. We began
        by noting that every summary document assignment (due the Friday following the seminar) should contain the following elements:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item the name of the presenter,
            \item the presentation title, and
            \item a thorough but consise summary.
        \end{itemize}
        The class expectations follow as:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item be on time,
            \item pay attention,
            \item ask questions.
        \end{itemize}
        The goals for the course in general follow as:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item to discover the research interests of a collection of faculty members,
            \item to experience a fairly broad range of research topics,
            \item to experience a broad range of presentation styles, and finally
            \item to gain a better understanding of what research is and how to do it.
        \end{itemize}
        When doing guided research with a professor there are a few expectations that go along with the hours obtained.
        The following give a fairly comprehensive set of expectations for research operation:
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item work consistently,
            \item meeting regularly,
            \item explain your results to others (for example in seminars),
            \item training (e.g. lab policies, software),
            \item reading,
            \item getting frustrated a lot.
        \end{enumerate}
        Some general advice follows as:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item pick something that you are passionate about,
            \item try to find a professor that you are comfortable with,
            \item pick something that is in an area of strength
            \item \textbf{think on your own}
            \item enjoy yourself!
        \end{itemize}
        After this description of the courses logistics and goals Dr. Chan got into his individual areas of research,
        which we'll address in the following sections.

        \section{Project 1. Rippled Dynamics}
        The idea at hand here is to study these sequence generators mainly for the purpose of modeling drug
        interactions with epilepsy patients. Though the lens through which we are looking at these drug interactions
        is differnce equations in the discrete dynamical system field. The curiousity with regards to these sequence
        generating functions is that they are both chaotic and periodic with their behaviour.

        The model that we were given follows as
        \begin{align*}
            x_{n+1} = \max\left\{\frac{A_n + D_n H_{\epsilon}(x_n)}{x_n}, \frac{B_n}{x_n-1}\right\}
        \end{align*}
        where $A_n$ and $D_n$ are periodic generating sequences, $B_n$ is non-periodic, and $H_{\epsilon}(x)$
        is the heavyside function. We generally simplify the model to:
        \begin{align*}
            x_{n+1} = \frac{A_n + D_n H_{\epsilon}(x_n)}{x_n}
        \end{align*}
        We then proceeded to go over graphs of these models showing periodic behaviour, and then with persistent
        iterations mild non-periodic behaviour develops over top of the persistent periodic behaviour.

        \section{Project 2. Barrier Island Evolution}
        Barrier islands, take for example Parramore Island on the Eastern Shore of Virginia, have quite a subtly
        non-stable landmass. So we're attempting to model the island topograpy over time with regards to both physical and
        biological stimuli (e.g. tides, storms, wave mechanics, plants, grasses). Specifically noted as the modeling
        mechanics were Cellular Models/ABM. Furthermore, the mechanics of the models heavily rely upon linear algebra.
        The final goal with the project would be to eventually develop a classification system upon which a risk
        metric would be developed (in the same sense as the risk metric for hurricanes).

        \section{Project 3. Cortisol Modeling}
        Using ordinary differential equations the goal is to create a model of the hormone cortisol in the bloodstream.
        The motivation for such a model is that it is well known in the medical community that cortisol is the hormone
        responsible for stress. Further, we have the goal here of modeling epinephrine and norepinephrine in the body
        to measure patient status in the hospital. From what I could tell, these models are in fairly late stages and
        the work is in the fine-tuning them. Lastly, there is a goal with the work to potentially implement the models
        in devices that would monitor these hormonal levels and process the models for patient classification for
        the potential of alerting or automated intervention. Granted, it sounded like the clinical reasarch is a bit off
        and the ODE work is primary.

        \section{Project 4. Hospital Epidemiology}
        This seemed like a fairly expansive research topic using a variety of means at getting traction on different
        mechanisms of disease spread. The different means of modeling listed were: ODEs, Markov chains, ABM, graphs,
        and more. Some of the problems that we are gain traction on are:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item disease transmission via bathing wipes
            \item patterns of disease spread with COVID where there is a comorbidity of chlamydia
            \item transmission of clostridioides difficile (a highly contageous pathogen via contact)
        \end{itemize}

        The first and most emphasized point here was that humains are extraordinarily complex, but also that humans

        modeling to using graph theory to gain traction on the problems.
